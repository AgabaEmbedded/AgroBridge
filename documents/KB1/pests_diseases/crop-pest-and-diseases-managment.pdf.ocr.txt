Preventive Measures This involves steps taken before attack to create an unfavorable environment for pests/pathogens, reducing the likelihood of attack. To achieve this, fanners should consider several key steps. Understanding the cropping history of the selected site is crucialtoanticipating potential pest and disease problems. It is advisable to avoid sites known to harbor pests targeting the crop of interest. Sourcing pest/disease resistant planting materials from trusted sources prevents severe damage. Pre-planting treatments, such as seed treatments for crops prone to early seedling infestations/infections are also effective. Additionally, planning planting times to avoid peak pest/pathogen and weed - seasons helps prevent infestations. Control Measures B. Chemical method: Bordeaux mixture or the use of copper-based fungicide is highly effective for most fungal diseases. For insects, suitable chemicals following manufacturer's recommendation should be applied. Some common insecticides are cypermethrin and lambdacyhalothrin. Weeds control may be achieved using chemicals, applied before (pre-emergence) or after (post-emergence) crop growth. These chemicals work either systemically (internal) or through contact (surface). In the case of nematode infestation, nematicides can be applied to control nematode populations. However, their use should be managed carefully to avoid environmental harm and resistance build up. Physical Method: Soil solarization (using plastic sheets to trap solar heat), and mechanical removal of infected plants (rogueing) can help physically reduce nematodes insects, fungi and weeds populations. This is especially effective in small-scale farrmng. This method is eco-friendly and very effective. Cultural Methods; Crop rotation and intercropping practices may be used to disrupt pest and pathogen life cycles and enhance soil health, Use of cover crops and mulches helps shade out weeds and reduce their growth.
